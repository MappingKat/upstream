Technology Stack — Layer by Layer
① Field / OT Layer — Physical Assets
On-ground
Pumps & Lift Stations
Move water through distribution or to treatment. Primary energy cost.
Sensors
Flow, pressure, pH, chlorine, turbidity, DO. Often analog; many are decades old.
Valves & Controls
Manual or electric. Isolation, pressure regulation, flow direction.
Treatment Systems
Chlorination, filtration, UV, aeration. Chemicals dosed manually or via PLC.
Meters (AMI/AMR)
Customer-side. Smart meters increasingly deployed but rollouts are slow for small utilities.
② Edge / Communication Layer
RTU / PLC
RTUs / PLCs
Remote Terminal Units or Programmable Logic Controllers. Read sensors, send commands. The "brain" at each site.
Communication Links
Cellular (4G/LTE), radio, or sometimes still dial-up modems for rural sites. Connectivity is patchy.
Local HMI Panels
Touchscreen or button panel at each pump station. Often the only on-site interface.
③ SCADA / Control Center
Core Platform
SCADA Software
Central system that aggregates all RTU data. Vendors: Ignition, OSIsoft PI, GE iFIX, Wonderware, or small cloud SaaS (e.g. High Tide, eLynx).
HMI Dashboards
Operator-facing screens. Show system state, alarms, trends. Often proprietary per-vendor.
Alarm Management
Alerts on overflows, pressure loss, equipment failure. Often very noisy; operators get alarm fatigue.
Data Historian
Time-series database logging sensor readings. This is the raw material for any AI/ML work.
④ IT / Business Systems
Enterprise
GIS
Geographic mapping of all pipes, assets, zones. Often disconnected from SCADA.
CMMS / Asset Mgmt
Work orders, maintenance logs, equipment records. Many small utilities use spreadsheets.
Billing / CIS
Customer billing, meter reads, revenue tracking. Often a separate vendor entirely.
Compliance / Reporting
EPA & state reporting. Manual forms, spreadsheets, or basic software. High-stakes, error-prone.
⚠ Known Gaps & Pain Points
Where AI fits
No Data Integration
SCADA, GIS, CMMS, and billing are all silos. Nobody sees the full picture.
Proprietary Lock-in
Legacy systems use proprietary protocols. Upgrading is slow and expensive (3–10 year cycles).
Noisy Alarms, No Predictions
Operators react to failures. No predictive capability. No anomaly detection at scale.
No IT Staff
Most small utilities have zero dedicated IT. Any new tech must be nearly zero-config.
Sparse Sensor Coverage
Many remote sites have minimal instrumentation — especially in distribution networks.
The Daily Reality

👤 The Operator's World

⏰
On-call 24/7. A single operator may cover the entire system — drinking water treatment, pumping, and sometimes wastewater too.
📱
Phone alarms at 3 AM. SCADA pings when something is off. The operator drives out to check. Often a false alarm.
📋
Manual logs everywhere. Paper logbooks at the plant, spreadsheets for compliance, sticky notes for reminders.
🔧
Fix it when it breaks. Reactive maintenance is the norm. Predictive is aspirational.
🎓
Institutional knowledge walks out the door. When a veteran retires, decades of "know-how" disappear.

📊 The Manager's Constraints

💰
Tight budgets, big decisions. A $50K software purchase is a serious capital decision. ROI must be clear and fast.
📜
Regulatory pressure is rising. New PFAS rules, cybersecurity mandates, and EPA compliance deadlines create urgency.
🏛️
Slow procurement. Public entities follow bureaucratic purchasing rules. Sales cycles are long.
🌊
Aging infrastructure. Average U.S. water pipe is 45+ years old. Failures are unpredictable and costly.
🔗
Grant-driven investment. Federal and state funding (Infrastructure Investment & Jobs Act) is the main path to new tech.
🎯

The AI Opportunity Window

Nearly half of utilities cite staffing shortages as the #1 barrier to adopting digital solutions — which is exactly the paradox AI is positioned to break. These utilities have SCADA data sitting in historians, going mostly unanalyzed. They have no ML capability in-house. They desperately need predictive maintenance, anomaly detection, and automated compliance — but they need it delivered as a near-zero-config cloud service, priced affordably, and proven fast. The data is already flowing. The need is urgent. What's missing is the software layer that turns raw SCADA time-series into actionable intelligence for a 3-person team.
